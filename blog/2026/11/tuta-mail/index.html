Tuta Mail

Posted: 2026-11-18   |   Tags: blog, email, privacy, Tuta, Tutanota

Readers of this blog will notice a trend that I sometimes only sit down to write when I get worked up about something, often from having to adapt to something I am used to becoming broken, and then finding new solutions. This is one of those posts, so skip if you are not interested in a personal experience of Tuta (Tutanota) mail.

I will be writing more, and in more general terms, in upcoming about a theme that I have decided to put front and center in my work and personal life, which I will be referring to as “Taking Privacy Seriously”, so stay tuned for that.

Back to Tuta.

Background

Back in 2018, I think, I signed up for the then Tutanota, before its name shortening, attracted by their privacy commitment, their German location, and their somewhat less commercial feel than their leading competitor Protonmail. If you’re interested in this, you probably know who these companies are, or could find other posts that would better inform you about their history and features.

Here I’d like to reflect more on the specific issues that led me to realize that I had been the proverbial frog in gradually heating water, my initial unproblematic experience blinding me to both what I was missing and a gradually degrading experience. Let’s jump in (oh wait, I’m the frog, already in the water).

Tutanota issues

Spam filter

Spam filtering had always been a little clunky in Tuta because the e-mails are encrypted and cannot be inspected the same way. But, configure some rules and in general it could be set up to work decently.

But in 2026, obviously Tuta had bolted on some new, poorly implemented, AI spam filter, since the behavior of the system changed dramatically in recent months, with LOTS of things approved that were spam, and lots of legitimate content being labelled spam. This included regular correspondents and services of mine. If I’m e-mailing an address regularly, their incoming mail shouldn’t be labelled spam! You’d think that would be basic.

One notable example being the daily USPS Informed Delivery e-mail going to spam. How this could be labelled spam is indicative of severe defects in the Tuta spam filtering, since this is easily identifiable as official content, and maintains consistent formatting. It going to spam must be the result of idiot AI looking at some specific metric, like the number of e-mails going out, or the number left unread, rather than smarter rule-based filtering. The kicker is that REPEATEDLY hitting the “Not Spam” button on these important messages did nothing to fix the problem, and trying to label spam as spam was only marginally more effective. This was rolled out to customers as a “surprise/gotcha” kind of feature, without any explanation or documentation. After experiencing problems, I was able to locate some more information about what Tuta was doing elsewhere on the internet, but that was after I had already experienced the pain. Tuta needed to do better at both its implementation and communication with customers.

Font size

I have pleaded on a couple of separate occasions with Tuta support for some kind of app-native font adjustment and selection. The app and web versions are just not readable on some devices with eyes like mine. I was told to use the browser or phone settings. But making global changes like that affects interactions with other apps and sites that DO NOT HAVE Tuta’s legibility problems. The cramped and crowded Tuta layout is dramatically worse in legibility than ANY of the other numerous mail apps I use, from the company-specific interfaces like Proton or Startmail, to clients like Thunderbird (phone and desktop), Claws, and others. How an accessibility and usability issue like that can not be a major priority is also a mystery to me. Another area where Tuta’s responsiveness is poor, and a subtle arrogance towards their customers manifests itself.

Tutanota -> Tuta migration botched

When Tutanota rebranded to Tuta, the message to existing customers like me was “just relax, all of your services will remain the same”. Now, true to the letter of that committment, my existing service and plan price were grandfathered in. But, some of the spirit of this implicit promise has been lacking recently. New features, like importing email in bulk, are not available on legacy plans and requires a switch to the new plans (now several years old). The new plans, given the combination of features that I use, are much more expensive, and switching would involve actually losing features. And while a few years ago, the decision to leave legacy users on tutanota.de email addresses seemed fine, the entire trend of the company towards “Tuta”-naming conventions has by now left legacy customers like me out in the cold.

Tuta could have taken an inclusive approach to bring their long-time customers along, but instead I am left feeling like an unwanted bother. Obviously the future portends more austerity for the legacy customers, so better to bail out now.

Potential delivery issues

Through researching my Tuta problems, I discovered that there has been an ongoing issue, I am not sure how extensive, of Gmail flagging all Tuta-origin mail as spam, at least for some users. See this reddit post. Yet another concern I had been woefully ignorant of as the water heated around me in the pot.

App does not display all mail

Worst of all, the app behaved inconsistently. There were little problems that I just lived with, like the fact that somehow my contacts multiplied over time until I had about 8 copies of each person in my Contacts list. Is this because I was using the app across multiple phones and devices? Certainly a product of bugs or bad design, but just an annoyance. However, one big problem pushed me over the edge.

In my Tuta app on one phone, all e-mails in a folder were shown. In the Tuta app on another phone, two very important e-mails from that folder were missing, not displaying in the list at all (but somehow showed up in search). I could not get them to show even with multiple reboots and restarts. You HAVE to be able to trust the basic functionality of your e-mail service, and that e-mails would not be lost, but that trust was now gone for me.

This is when I panicked about losing important content and realized that I NEEDED IMAP and local copies on my devices. Handing over complete trust to this opaque and slightly malfunctioning service, including never having the ability to decrypt or access content without reliance on them, given their custom, non-standard form of e-mail encryption, suddenly loomed as a much larger issue.

Now probably someone from Tuta might give me some instructions to completely tear down and reinstall many things on my system to get the Tuta app to work correctly. But I maintain that this level of dysfunction should NEVER happen on such a basic part of the infrastructure of our digital lives.

I realized that Tuta is a company that is fundamentally at odds with some of my core computing values derived from the Linux and Open Source world. Namely, that the user should remain in control, and that the software should be as transparent and granular as possible, not in a siloed warehouse controlled by someone else. Being able to move e-mails in and out of the service itself is a major part of that. With this week’s internet outages, that principle is more important than ever.

I am complaining a lot here, but I do want to recognize that until quite recently, Tuta functioned quite well for me, and has been a strong advocate for privacy in general. However, it no longer fits my current concerns.
